# StreamVault Licensing Analysis — Open Questions

## 1. Purpose

The current analysis uses TMDb data to screen candidate movies and compare franchise-classified titles with standalone films.

It provides historical performance evidence, but it does not contain the commercial, contractual or audience information required to approve a licensing deal.

This document separates findings already established by the analysis from questions that require further investigation before a final acquisition decision.

## 2. Questions addressed by the analysis

| Question | Current finding |
|---|---|
| Do franchise-classified movies have higher median reported revenue than standalone movies? | Yes. Franchise medians are higher in both the all-years and 2000-onward comparisons. |
| Do franchise-classified movies have higher median gross-to-budget ratios? | Yes. The franchise median is higher in both periods. |
| Does the comparison change when only movies released from 2000 onward are considered? | The overall direction does not change, although the size of the differences changes. |
| Which franchise collections rank highly by median revenue? | The Avengers, The Dark Knight and Pirates of the Caribbean collections are the top three in the current candidate data. |
| Does an alternative franchise ranking produce the same shortlist? | No. Ranking by median gross-to-budget ratio changes the order and brings collections such as Star Wars and The Lord of the Rings to the top. |
| Were all candidate IDs successfully retrieved? | No. Of 117 valid extraction candidates, 112 returned both movie details and credits. Five returned HTTP 404 responses from both endpoints. |
| Do the four sampled records match current live TMDb responses? | Yes. The four-film spot-check found 32 matching field comparisons and no differences. This is a limited sample, not full validation. |

These findings support further screening and investigation. They do not establish licensing availability, commercial value or expected streaming performance.

## 3. Questions for studios and rights holders

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Are the shortlisted movies currently available for licensing? | A strong-performing title is not useful if the rights cannot be acquired. |
| Are the titles offered individually or only as a bundle? | This determines whether StreamVault can choose specific films or must accept a package. |
| What is the quoted licensing price for each title or bundle? | The current analysis has no acquisition prices, so it cannot establish whether a deal is affordable or attractive. |
| What territories are included in the rights? | Rights may not cover every market where StreamVault operates. |
| How long does the licence last? | Licence duration affects the value StreamVault can obtain. |
| Are there exclusivity restrictions or competing platform rights? | These may affect the commercial value of the agreement. |
| Are there minimum guarantees, renewal conditions or other contractual obligations? | These can increase the actual commitment beyond the headline price. |

## 4. Questions for StreamVault's audience and content teams

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Have StreamVault subscribers watched or searched for these titles or similar films? | Historical cinema revenue does not prove demand among StreamVault subscribers. |
| Which titles are likely to attract new subscribers or reduce cancellations? | Acquisition decisions should connect to StreamVault's business objectives. |
| Does StreamVault already hold rights to any shortlisted titles? | Existing rights may make a new purchase unnecessary. |
| Which territories and subscriber groups show the strongest demand? | Demand may differ across markets. |
| What viewing or engagement targets would make a proposed deal successful? | These targets provide a way to assess the deal after acquisition. |

## 5. Questions for Finance

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is the maximum amount available within the $14 million acquisition budget? | The budget request does not establish how much can be committed to each deal. |
| What additional costs should be included beyond the licence fee? | The licence fee alone may not represent the full cost of acquiring and distributing the content. |
| What financial return or performance threshold must a deal meet? | The team needs an agreed basis for comparing the cost with expected value. |
| How should historical box-office figures be treated when comparing older and newer films? | The current figures are not adjusted for inflation and are not licensing returns. |

## 6. Questions about data quality and scope

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Can the original source of the five IDs returning HTTP 404 be checked? | The records may be incorrect, unavailable or mapped to another identifier. |
| Can the reported budget and revenue figures for shortlisted titles be verified against more authoritative sources? | TMDb figures have not been independently verified. |
| Should a franchise be defined using TMDb collections, studio bundle offers or another agreed business definition? | A database collection is not necessarily a licensable bundle. |
| Is the current candidate list representative of the films StreamVault can actually acquire? | The results describe this candidate list, not the entire film market. |

## 7. Evidence required before final approval

The acquisition team should obtain:

1. Written availability and rights information from the relevant studios.
2. Actual licence quotes and full contractual terms.
3. StreamVault viewing, search and subscriber engagement evidence.
4. Finance's assessment of the full acquisition cost and required performance threshold.
5. Verification of important financial figures and unresolved candidate IDs.
6. An agreed definition of commercial franchise bundles and a clear method for comparing individual-title and bundle offers.

## 8. Current decision boundary

The current evidence supports using franchise performance as a screening signal. Franchise-classified movies have higher median reported revenue and gross-to-budget ratios in both periods examined.

However, the alternative ranking shows that the preferred collections depend on the performance measure used. The Avengers ranks first by median revenue, while Star Wars ranks first among the collections meeting the two-film minimum when ranked by median gross-to-budget ratio.

The analysis does not establish that a franchise bundle is the best $14 million purchase. It cannot calculate a defensible offer price, expected streaming return or final deal value without the missing commercial and audience evidence.

**Recommendation:** Use the current shortlist to guide further investigation and request comparable offers. Evaluate the price, rights, duration, audience demand and contractual obligations of each offer before making a commitment. Do not treat historical box-office comparisons as approval to spend the acquisition budget.
